Seven steps to faster claims handling with an AI agent

Nothing in my setup is exotic, and that is the point. If you run claims at an insurer with a core system and an IT team that can call an API, you can follow the same path. Here is what I would tell you to do.

1

Measure the checking first

Sit with your handlers for a week and log how much time goes into document completeness and cross-checking versus actual decisions. For me it was the 20–25% that later disappeared.

Who: head of claims
Illustrative questions

Look at the data in your claims system and talk to your handlers. These are the answers you are looking for.

  1. How many documents arrive per claim, and in how many separate deliveries?
  2. In what share of claims is a required document missing at first submission?
  3. How many times does a handler open one claim before it is closed?
  4. How long does a newly arrived document wait before someone looks at it?
  5. What share of the time on a claim goes into checking, and what share into deciding?

Keep the answers. They are your “before” numbers.

Pick one product line

Start with the line that has the highest volume and the most standardised document set. I started with MTPL (motor third-party liability).

Who: head of claims
3

Write down the document set and the rules

For that line, define which documents are required, which facts must agree across them, and what counts as a contradiction. This becomes the agent's configuration in ClaimSense.

Who: your most experienced handlers
Illustrative example · any product line

Every claim has to answer the same seven questions. Write down where each answer appears and what must hold.

QuestionWhere the answer appearsWhat must hold
Who is claiming?Claim form, policy, ID or supporting documentsSame person or company everywhere. If it is not the policyholder, the relationship is stated
What is insured?Policy, claim form, supporting documentsAll refer to the same vehicle, property, trip or person
When did it happen?Claim form, third-party reportSame date, and inside the policy period
Where did it happen?Claim form, third-party reportConsistent, and inside the territory covered
What happened?Claim form, third-party report, photosThe descriptions do not contradict each other
How much is claimed?Estimate, invoice, receiptsAmounts add up, and any difference from the estimate is explained
Who gets paid?Claim form, invoice, bank detailsPayee is the claimant or a named service provider

The rows translate to each line. “What is insured” is the registration number in motor, the booking in travel and the patient in health. Add a short list next to the table: the documents a claim of this type cannot move forward without.

4

Wire the API

Add a call from your core claims system to ClaimSense on every document arrival, and a place in the claim screen to show the health check. Keep your core system as the source of truth.

Who: IT builds it. Compliance and data protection sign it off.
Illustrative scope

Two things to build.

  1. The core system sends the new document together with the claim context.
  2. It stores and displays what comes back: the claim's status, the list of missing documents and the list of contradictions.

Before the first real claim is sent: claim files hold personal data, sometimes health data. Have data protection, information security and your outsourcing rules reviewed first, and keep the agent's findings in the claim's audit trail.

5

Run it in shadow mode

Let the agent check claims for a few weeks while your handlers still do their own checks. Compare. Tune the rules and thresholds until your team trusts the findings.

Who: handlers and head of claims
Illustrative comparison

For each claim, put the handler's findings next to the agent's.

OutcomeWhat it tells youWhat to do
Both found the issueThe rule worksNothing
Only the agent, and it was rightThis is your gainCount it
Only the agent, and it was wrongThe rule is too strictLoosen it
Only the handlerA rule is missingAdd it

A typical false alarm: one document says “1AB 2345” and another says “1AB2345”. It is the same registration number, so the rule is adjusted to ignore spaces.

6

Make the health check the starting point of every case

Once the trust is there, your handlers stop re-checking files by hand and start each case from the agent's findings. The decision on the claim stays with them. Redirect the freed time explicitly to complex claims and client contact, and tell your team that is the plan.

Who: head of claims
Illustrative check

Answer the five questions from step 1 again after a few months and compare.

In hindsight

What I learned along the way

Pick a task people are happy to give away.Adoption was never an issue for me because nobody missed the checking.
Do not build a parallel system.Because the agent lives inside the claim, it is used on every case by default.
Configure per product line from day one.One generic checker would have been too strict for some lines and too loose for others.
Keep model choice in your own hands.Being able to swap models later removed a whole category of vendor-risk objections from my board conversations.
Measure before and after.A concrete 20–25% figure did more for my business case than any slide about AI.

A closing word

I did not innovate Pillow's claims operation by buying a big platform or launching a transformation programme. I found the one piece of work my people were quietly losing a quarter of their day to, and I gave it to an agent that never sleeps. Everything else stayed the same, which is exactly why it worked.

If you are facing the same choice I was, hire more people or think differently, I hope this account helps you choose the second one. I am happy to compare notes with any operations leader who wants to try it.

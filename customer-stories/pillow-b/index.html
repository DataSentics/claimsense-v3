Scaling Claims Without Scaling Headcount

How I put an AI agent inside every claim at Pillow and gave my handlers a quarter of their time back.

Why I am writing this

I am Marcel Beno, COO of Pillow, a fast-growing challenger insurer. A year ago I was facing the classic operations problem: my claims volume was growing faster than I could sensibly hire. Today my claim handlers spend 20–25% less time per case, because I put an always-on AI agent inside every claim.

The agent has one job: to check that every document arriving in a claim is complete, correct and consistent with the rest of the file. It runs 24/7 and keeps a live health check on each claim, so my people spend their days on the cases that need judgment, negotiation and empathy.

We kept our standard core claims system and call ClaimSense as an API from inside it. I pick the AI models and tune the agent separately for each product line, from MTPL (motor third-party liability) to Casco (cover for damage to your own vehicle).

The problem on my desk

We had just brought claims handling in-house after years with an external partner. That gave us control over the client experience, the data and the speed of every decision. It also made the claims team my cost and my bottleneck.

At the same time, Pillow was growing fast, and every policy we sold was a future claim. If claims grew 40% a year, did my claims team have to grow 40% a year too?

I saw three options:

If claims grow 40% a year, does the team grow 40% too?
Option 1
Hire more and more people.

Predictable, but slow and expensive. Good handlers are scarce, and a new hire needs months to become productive.

Option 2
Let service slip.

Cheap in the short term and fatal for a challenger whose pitch is being better than the incumbents.

Option 3 · My choice
Think differently.

Find the part of a handler's day that does not need a human, and hand it to a machine.

So I sat with the team. A large share of every handler's day was not decision-making at all. It was checking. Is the police report there? Does the registration number match across the claim form, the photos and the repair estimate? What is still missing before this case can move?

A large share of every handler's day was not decision-making at all. It was checking.

This work matters, but it is repetitive, rule-based and constantly interrupting. Every arriving document means someone opens the file, re-reads it and re-checks it. It was the obvious place to start with an AI agent.

What I decided

I decided to hand one clearly bounded part of the claims process to AI agents: the factual completeness and cross-validation of client documents. Everything else, from liability assessment to reserving and settlement, stayed with my people.

I set myself three rules:

1
Start with the boring part.

My first AI use case had to be something my handlers were glad to give up. Nobody joined Pillow to compare registration numbers across PDFs.

2
Do not touch the core system.

Our claims system holds the process, the audit trail and all our integrations. The AI had to plug into what we already had.

3
Put the agent inside the claim, not beside it.

I did not want another tool to open. The agent runs inside each claim and shows its findings on the screen my handlers already use.

With those rules, the choice was quick. I chose ClaimSense, an AI claims solution called as an API from a core claims system. It gave me an agent per claim, an untouched core system, and separate behaviour per product line without new code.

How it works today

Every claim in our core system now has its own AI agent, from the moment the claim is opened until it is closed. It keeps asking one question: is this claim complete and consistent right now?

What the agent checks on every document arrival

A live health check inside the claim screen they already know: green when the file is complete and consistent, amber with a plain-language list of gaps or contradictions when it is not. Nobody re-reads the file to find out what changed.

Why 24/7 was the whole point for me

Documents arrive whenever clients, garages and police stations send them. Before, each one waited in a queue until a handler had time to re-check the case. Now the check is done by the time my team starts the day, and a handler's first action on a case is a decision.

Where the freed time went

I did not use the 20–25% to cut headcount. I used it to absorb growth and to raise quality. My handlers now spend more time on:

  • Complex and contested claims, where experience and negotiation skill change the outcome.
  • Direct contact with clients, instead of chasing paperwork.
  • Suspected fraud and edge cases, where a flagged contradiction needs a person to investigate.

What it meant for my growth question

My claims team now handles more claims per person. The team still grows, but at a fraction of the rate of the claims volume, and the people who join spend their first weeks learning to decide, not to check.

The point was never to remove people from claims. It was to stop wasting their time on something a machine does better.

Why it was easier than I expected: ClaimSense as an API

The project stayed small because ClaimSense fits into our core claims system instead of replacing it. Three things made the difference.

1. It is just an API call from our core system

Connecting ClaimSense meant one outbound call when a document arrives and one place to display the findings. No handler logs into a second tool, and our core system remains the single source of truth.

2. I choose the models

ClaimSense is not locked to a single AI model. I choose which models the agent uses and can change them as models improve, prices move or compliance requirements shift. As a European insurer, I want control over where our data goes and which vendors we depend on.

3. I tune the behaviour per product line

An MTPL claim and a Casco claim do not need the same checks, and neither does a property or travel claim. I configure the agent for each claims process separately:

What I tuned the agent to check
MTPL
Motor third-party liability

  • Their own required-document sets
  • Validation rules and tolerance thresholds

Same API and integration, a different configuration per line. Adding a product line is configuration, not development.

If you want to do the same

Nothing in my setup is exotic. If you run claims at an insurer with a core system and an IT team that can call an API, you can follow the same path.

What I learned along the way

A closing word

I did not innovate Pillow's claims operation by buying a big platform or launching a transformation programme. I found the one piece of work my people were quietly losing a quarter of their day to, and I gave it to an agent that never sleeps. Everything else stayed the same, which is exactly why it worked.

If you are facing the same choice, I am happy to compare notes with any operations leader who wants to try it.
